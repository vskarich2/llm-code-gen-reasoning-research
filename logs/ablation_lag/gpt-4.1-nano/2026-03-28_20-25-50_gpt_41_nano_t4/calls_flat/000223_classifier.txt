## Root Cause
The function make_transfer_b_to_a_steps locks resource B first, then resource A, which is the opposite order of make_transfer_a_to_b_steps that locks A first, then B. When concurrent transfers execute in interleaved steps, one thread may lock A while the other locks B, and then each attempts to lock the resource held by the other, causing a deadlock.

## Failure Mechanism
In interleaved_transfers, the first transfer locks A, then the second transfer locks B. When the first transfer proceeds to lock B, it is blocked because B is already locked by the second transfer. Conversely, the second transfer attempts to lock A, which is held by the first transfer, leading both to wait indefinitely for each other, resulting in a deadlock.

## Broken Invariant
The sequence of acquiring locks in transfer steps must always follow a consistent global order to prevent deadlocks, specifically, either always lock A before B or always lock B before A, regardless of transfer direction.

## Fix Strategy
Change make_transfer_b_to_a_steps to acquire resource A before resource B, aligning the lock order with make_transfer_a_to_b_steps. This ensures that both transfer functions acquire locks in the same order, preventing circular wait conditions and thus eliminating deadlocks during concurrent execution.
