# Developer's Reasoning

## Root Cause
In the order_service.py's place_order function, when the payment.process() call raises a ValueError due to payment failure, the function re-raises the exception without executing the cleanup steps: releasing the reserved inventory and removing the corresponding audit entry. This causes the inventory reservation and audit log to remain inconsistent with the actual payment failure.

## Failure Mechanism
If payment.process() raises an exception (e.g., 'payment declined'), the except block in place_order does not execute any cleanup, leading to reserved inventory remaining reserved and the audit log retaining the entry for an order that failed to pay. This results in inventory and audit data being corrupted, with reservations not released and audit entries not removed after a payment failure.

## Broken Invariant
When a payment failure occurs during place_order, the reserved inventory for the order is not released, and the corresponding audit entry is not removed, causing the inventory reservation and audit log to be inconsistent with the actual payment status.

## Fix Strategy
Adding a finally block in place_order ensures that, regardless of whether process() succeeds or raises an exception, the reserved inventory is released and the audit entry is removed if the payment fails. This guarantees that the inventory and audit log are correctly rolled back upon payment failure.
