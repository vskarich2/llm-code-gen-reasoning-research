# Developer's Reasoning

## Root Cause
The function make_transfer_b_to_a_steps locks resource B first, then resource A, which is the opposite order of make_transfer_a_to_b_steps that locks A first, then B. When concurrent transfers execute in interleaved steps, one thread may lock A while the other locks B, and then each attempts to lock the resource held by the other, causing a deadlock.

## Failure Mechanism
In interleaved_transfers, the first transfer locks A, then the second transfer locks B. When the first transfer proceeds to lock B, it is blocked because B is already locked by the second transfer, which in turn is waiting to lock A, which is already locked by the first transfer. This circular wait causes a deadlock, preventing any further progress.

## Broken Invariant
The sequence of acquiring locks must be consistent across all transfer functions to prevent circular wait conditions, ensuring that no deadlock occurs during concurrent transfers.

## Fix Strategy
Change make_transfer_b_to_a_steps to acquire resource A before resource B, matching the lock order of make_transfer_a_to_b_steps. This enforces a global lock acquisition order, preventing circular wait and thus deadlocks during concurrent execution.
