## Root Cause
The execute_transfer function directly calls logging and audit functions (record_transfer_attempt, emit_transfer_event, emit_failure_alert) within its core business logic, mixing observability with state changes. This coupling makes it difficult to isolate the core transfer logic from side effects.

## Failure Mechanism
When execute_transfer is invoked, it performs the transfer steps and logs events simultaneously. If a transient failure occurs (e.g., during the emit_failure_alert call), the logging functions are invoked within the transfer logic, causing side effects to be tightly coupled with control flow. This can lead to inconsistent or incomplete observability data if errors happen during logging, or make testing the core transfer logic difficult due to side effects.

## Broken Invariant
The core transfer operation (debiting sender, crediting receiver) should be independent of logging and observability calls, ensuring that the transfer's business logic can be tested and reasoned about separately from side effects.

## Fix Strategy
I extracted the core transfer logic into a helper function that performs only the balance updates and transfer validation, removing all logging and audit calls from it. Then, I wrapped this helper in a new function that handles all observability calls before and after invoking the core transfer, ensuring separation of concerns and cleaner, more testable code.
