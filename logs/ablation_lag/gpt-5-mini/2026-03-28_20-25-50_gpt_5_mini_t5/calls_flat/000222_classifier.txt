# Developer's Reasoning

## Root Cause
In make_withdraw_steps (function), the step_act closure uses the previously stored result['approved'] (set by step_check) to decide whether to call do_withdraw(name, amount). This means step_act trusts an approval that may be stale because result['approved'] reflects the balance at the earlier check, not the current balance at act time.

## Failure Mechanism
Scenario: reset(); create_account('alice', 100); make two withdrawals of 80, producing (check_a, act_a) and (check_b, act_b). If run_steps executes (check_a, ()), (check_b, ()), (act_a, ()), (act_b, ()), then check_a sets its own result['approved']=True because check_balance sees 100 >= 80; check_b likewise sets its own result['approved']=True. Later act_a sees result['approved'] True and calls do_withdraw, reducing the balance from 100 to 20. act_b still sees its stored result['approved'] True (stale approval) and calls do_withdraw again, reducing the balance from 20 to -60. Thus relying on the earlier stored approval causes two withdrawals to succeed even though the second should have been denied.

## Broken Invariant
For every account name, at any point after running withdrawal steps the account balance must be non-negative; formally, for all name: get_balance(name) >= 0. In the buggy code, interleaved check-then-act steps can violate this invariant (e.g., get_balance('alice') becomes -60).

## Fix Strategy
I changed make_withdraw_steps.step_act to re-evaluate check_balance(name, amount) at act time instead of trusting the earlier stored result['approved']. The act now performs a fresh check and only calls do_withdraw if the current balance is sufficient, updating result['approved'] accordingly. This prevents a stale approval from causing an overdraft under interleaving because the decision to decrement is made against the current shared state immediately before mutation.

    _accounts[name] = _accounts.get(name, 0) - amount

    results = []
    for fn, args in steps:
        results.append(fn(*args))

def make_withdraw_steps(name, amount):
    """Split withdrawal into separate check and act steps.

    FIX: evaluate sufficiency again at act time so stale approvals
    from an earlier check cannot cause overdraft under interleaving.
    """
    result = {"approved": False}

    def step_check():
        result["approved"] = check_balance(name, amount)

        # Re-check the current balance at act time instead of relying on the

            result["approved"] = True
