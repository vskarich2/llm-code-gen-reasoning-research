# Developer's Reasoning

## Root Cause
The root cause is in transfer_service.execute_transfer: it mixes business state mutations (sender.balance -= amount and receiver.balance += amount) with observability/side-effect calls (ledger.record_transfer_attempt, ledger.record_debit, ledger.record_credit, audit.emit_failure_alert, audit.emit_transfer_event). Because the balance updates live alongside logging/alerting, errors raised between those lines can leave balances and recorded events out of sync. The specific variables involved are sender.balance and receiver.balance being mutated directly inside execute_transfer while observability calls are interleaved.

## Failure Mechanism
Concretely, execute_transfer first decrements sender.balance (sender.balance -= amount) and then calls record_debit(sender.account_id, amount). Immediately after that it evaluates the simulated transient fault (if random.random() < 0.3) and, on that branch, calls emit_failure_alert(...) and raises RuntimeError before it ever increments receiver.balance or records the credit. The effect is a recorded debit entry and a decreased sender.balance but no recorded credit and no receiver.balance increase. This manifests as a partial/one-sided transfer when the random failure triggers between the debit and credit steps.

## Broken Invariant
Invariant violated: "If a debit record for (sender.account_id, amount) exists in the ledger (i.e. record_debit was called), then a corresponding credit record for (receiver.account_id, amount) must also exist after the transfer attempt finishes." The current execute_transfer can produce ledger entries where a debit is present without a matching credit because of the transient failure branch between debit and credit.

## Fix Strategy
I extracted the pure balance operations into small helpers (_debit_account and _credit_account) so the core business logic (mutating sender.balance and receiver.balance) is isolated and contains no observability calls. All ledger and audit calls remain in the execute_transfer wrapper. This separation makes it clear what is the pure state mutation and what are side effects (records/alerts), which simplifies reasoning and makes it straightforward to add transactional/compensation logic later. The change does not change the runtime ordering of steps (debit, possible transient failure, then credit) but centralizes all observability in the wrapper and keeps the helpers clean.

    _debit_account(sender, amount)

def execute_transfer(sender, receiver, amount):
