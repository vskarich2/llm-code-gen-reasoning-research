## Root Cause
pipeline.ingest: the for-loop over 'range(max_pipeline_retries)' lacks a break after a successful send_with_retry call, causing repeated calls to send_with_retry (and therefore sender.send -> store.append) even after a successful send.

## Failure Mechanism
When ingest(msg, fail_first=True) is called: pipeline.ingest calls send_with_retry(msg). send_with_retry calls send(msg) which on a transient first-call failure raises ConnectionError and then succeeds on a subsequent retry. Because ingest's loop does not break after this success, ingest calls send_with_retry(msg) again on the next pipeline attempt. That second call to send invokes sender.send again, which appends the same message a second time to store._messages. This mutation (additional append) propagates to the public store.get_messages() and message_count(), producing duplicated messages visible to callers.

## Broken Invariant
For a single successful ingest(msg) call, store.message_count() increments by exactly 1 and store.get_messages() contains at most one copy of that message. (This invariant is violated: a single ingest can produce multiple appends of the same msg.)

## Fix Strategy
1) Ensure send_with_retry returns a boolean indicating whether a send actually succeeded (return True immediately on success, and return False after exhausting retries). This avoids masking persistent failures. 2) Modify pipeline.ingest to break out of its retry loop as soon as send_with_retry reports success, and only append to _ingest_log when a real send succeeded. 3) Fix pipeline.reset to call the module-level reset functions for store and sender instead of touching undefined module-level variables. Together these changes break the causal chain by (a) preventing pipeline from making additional send() calls after a successful send_with_retry, and (b) preventing pipeline from recording a message as ingested when no send actually succeeded.
