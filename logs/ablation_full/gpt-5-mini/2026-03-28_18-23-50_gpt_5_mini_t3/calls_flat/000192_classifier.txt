# Developer's Reasoning

## Root Cause
The function execute_transfer mutates the business state (sender.balance and receiver.balance) while interleaving observability calls (record_transfer_attempt, record_debit, record_credit, emit_transfer_event, emit_failure_alert). Because the balance updates (sender.balance -= amount and receiver.balance += amount) are mixed with logging/audit calls, a transient failure that occurs between those mutations leaves the system in a partially-applied state: sender.balance is changed while receiver.balance is not, and ledger/audit entries are inconsistent.

## Failure Mechanism
Concrete scenario: execute_transfer(sender, receiver, amount) runs and calls record_transfer_attempt(...). It then does sender.balance -= amount and calls record_debit(...). If random.random() < 0.3 the code executes emit_failure_alert(...) and raises RuntimeError("transient failure during credit") before executing receiver.balance += amount and record_credit(...). The observable outcome is: the sender's balance has been decreased, the ledger contains a debit entry and a transfer_attempt, the audit contains a failure alert, but there is no credit entry and the receiver's balance was never increased. This leaves total funds decreased by amount and ledger/audit inconsistent with account state.

## Broken Invariant
After a failed transfer that raises an exception, the sum of balances of the involved accounts must equal the sum before the transfer (i.e. sum([sender.balance, receiver.balance]) after failure == sum before); currently this invariant is violated when the transient error triggers between the debit and credit. Equivalently, every recorded debit entry must have a corresponding recorded credit entry (or a completed compensating action) — this is not guaranteed.

## Fix Strategy
I isolated the pure business operation that mutates account state into a small helper _adjust_balance(account, delta) so that balance changes are localized and free of logging. All observability (ledger writes and audit emits) are kept in the execute_transfer wrapper, which now orchestrates the order of operations and side effects. Concretely: the lines sender.balance -= amount and receiver.balance += amount were moved into the helper, and record_transfer_attempt, record_debit, record_credit, emit_failure_alert and emit_transfer_event are consolidated in execute_transfer. This refactor doesn't silently hide side effects — it makes the boundary between state mutation and observability explicit, which reduces the risk of further accidental interleaving and makes it straightforward to add transactional or compensating logic around the wrapper later.
