## Root Cause
In the create_config function, the variable 'config' is assigned directly to DEFAULTS, which is a dictionary. This means 'config' references the same dictionary object as DEFAULTS, so any modifications to 'config' (such as update) will also modify DEFAULTS, violating the intended immutability of the defaults.

## Failure Mechanism

Subsequent calls to create_config or reset_defaults will then operate on a mutated DEFAULTS, leading to unexpected default values or side effects across different parts of the program.

## Broken Invariant
The DEFAULTS dictionary remains unchanged after create_config is called with overrides, ensuring that defaults are always consistent and can be reset reliably by reset_defaults.

## Fix Strategy
By replacing 'config = DEFAULTS' with 'config = DEFAULTS.copy()', the function creates a shallow copy of DEFAULTS for each call, preventing modifications to the copy from affecting the original DEFAULTS dictionary. This preserves the integrity of DEFAULTS and ensures predictable behavior.

def create_config(overrides=None):
